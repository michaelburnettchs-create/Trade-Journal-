Trading model
Break & Retest model — the rules every trade is judged against.
1. Overview 2. Market context 3. Key levels 4. Mechanics 5. Entry rules 6. Stop loss 7. Exit & TP 8. Risk mgmt 9. Checklist 10. Mental framework 11. Trade review

1. Model Overview

The Break & Retest model is a level-reactive system. Price is not predicted — it is observed. I wait for price to break a key level, return to it, and demonstrate through its behavior that the level holds. Only then is an entry taken.

Core principle: I react to what price does at a level. I do not anticipate. Entry direction is determined by price structure — not by bias, trend, or expectation.

This model trades both sides. Previous support can become resistance; previous rejection can become demand. Counter-trend entries are fully valid when the level is high-trust and the entry is tight. Ticker selection is entirely discretionary — any name that demonstrates respect for key levels is in play.

2. Market Context & Correlation

NQ, ES, SPY, and QQQ are required confluence checks before every trade. They function as a gate, not just additive context.

Required pre-trade check

  • Assess NQ and ES for overall market direction and key levels before session open
  • SPY and QQQ provide intraday confirmation — is the broader market respecting or breaking structure?
  • Market alignment with the trade direction adds conviction. Market disagreement requires a stronger level to justify entry.
  • For counter-trend entries: if NQ/ES/SPY/QQQ are in strong directional trend against the trade, the level must be exceptional. Borderline levels in this context are a hard pass.
Market correlation is a required gate — not optional context. If the broader market picture has not been assessed, no entries are taken.

Futures as trade vehicles

NQ and ES are occasionally traded directly when a clear break-and-retest setup presents itself. All entry, confirmation, stop, and exit rules apply identically to futures positions.

3. Key Level Identification

A level without prior respect is not a level. Every trade is anchored to a zone where price has previously reacted in a meaningful, identifiable way.

Timeframes

  • Level identification: 30-minute and 4-hour charts
  • Intraday structure and trend context: 15-minute chart
  • 2-minute chart: the primary trading timeframe — all entry decisions, confirmation, and stop placement are executed here
  • The level must show respect on the 2-minute chart. If price is not behaving cleanly at the level on the 2-minute, the entry is not valid regardless of higher timeframe structure.
  • Stop placement: 2-minute close below (long) or above (short) the key level

What qualifies as a key level

  • Prior significant reaction: rejection, breakout, or consolidation zone that is clearly visible
  • EMA confluence: Daily 20, 50, 100, 200 — alignment with a moving average strengthens the level
  • Session structure: PMH, PML, prior day highs/lows, VWAP
  • 4-hour PATH levels and established demand/supply zones
  • Multiple prior touches increase conviction. A level respected four times is stronger than one respected once.
  • Cross-timeframe alignment — level visible and respected on both 30min and 4hr — is highest priority.
  • Level must show respect on the 2-minute chart — clean wicking or consolidation at the level on the execution timeframe is required.

Ticker selection

Any ticker is in play at full discretion. The only requirement is demonstrated respect for key levels and options contracts that move cleanly enough to trade. Familiarity with how a ticker's contracts move — spread behavior, delta response, fill quality — determines position sizing, not eligibility.

  • Full size: tickers whose contract movement and fill behavior are well understood
  • Reduced size (50–75%): first trades on a name, or after a period away from it
  • Wide spreads change effective RR — always factor spread into sizing and TP calculation before entry

4. Trade Mechanics

Step 1 — The Break

Wait for price to make a clear, decisive break through a key level. The break is setup identification only. No entry is taken here.

Step 2 — The Retest

Price must return to the broken level and demonstrate that the level holds. Confirmation is observed on the 2-minute chart.

  • Standard: minimum 4 minutes of level holding
  • Exception: sub-4 minutes is allowed only when the level is very high-trust — multiple prior touches, strong prior reaction — AND the entry is exceptionally tight
  • This exception exists for situations where waiting for full confirmation means losing the entry entirely. It is not a shortcut for weak setups.
  • Confirmation signals: wicking at the level, doji candles, failed breakdowns or breakouts, consolidation
  • Additional retests strengthen the setup — the more a level is tested and holds, the higher the conviction
The 4-minute hold rule filters fakeouts and liquidity sweeps. Every time it is bypassed without a genuinely strong level behind it, it costs capital.

Step 3 — Entry

Entry is triggered on the engulfing candle confirming rejection or reclaim of the level, after confirmation criteria are met.

  • Tight entry: stop is placed at the level, targeting minimal drawdown from entry
  • Entry is off the engulfing candle — not chased after it has extended
  • The reason to enter must be nameable before the order is placed: wicking, doji, consolidation, or confirmed reclaim

6. Stop Loss

The stop defines the exact point where the trade idea is invalidated. It is defined before entry and is never moved against the position.

Standard stop

  • Trigger: 2-minute close below the key level (long) or above the key level (short)
  • This is the invalidation point — price has disproven the setup

Allowed stop adjustments

  • Move to breakeven after TP1 is hit — always valid, eliminates downside on runners
  • Trailing stop on 8EMA 2-minute close — valid at any point as a structural trade management tool
  • Price structure trailing using HH/HL (longs) or LH/LL (shorts) — use whichever structure price is currently respecting
Stop adjustments are structural trade management decisions made based on price behavior. They are completely separate from the daily loss rule and have nothing to do with closing early.

7. Exit & Take Profit

Structured exit rule

Every exit must be triggered by one of the following structural conditions. Any close outside of these is an emotional exit and a rule violation.

✓
Stop hit at invalidation level
2-minute close beyond the key level — trade idea is invalidated.
✓
Take profit level reached
Price reaches a pre-marked TP level. Scale or close according to the plan set before entry.
✓
Trailing stop triggered
8EMA 2-minute close, price structure trail (HH/HL or LH/LL), or breakeven stop hit on a runner. The trail mechanism was pre-committed before or during the trade — not invented in the moment.
×
Closing out of P&L anxiety
Manually closing a position because it is profitable and you are nervous — without a structural trigger. This is the primary documented P&L leak.
×
Closing to avoid a potential loss
Exiting before the stop is hit because the trade "feels" like it is going wrong. If the stop has not been hit, the idea has not been invalidated.

Take profit structure

  • TP1, TP2, and TP3 are marked on the chart before entry — based on prior liquidity zones, not percentage targets
  • After TP1 is hit: move stop to breakeven, let remaining position run to TP2/TP3
  • Partial scales at each TP level are valid — the runner stays in until a structural exit triggers

Time-of-day rule (0DTE)

On 0DTE positions, theta decay accelerates materially after 11:15 AM ET. The exit mechanism shifts accordingly.

  • Before 11:15 AM: hold to pre-marked TP levels unless a structural stop triggers first
  • After 11:15 AM on 0DTE: 8EMA trailing stop (2-min close) becomes the primary exit — time decay is now working against holding for a distant TP
  • Longer-dated contracts: this cutoff is less relevant — decay is slower and TP levels can be held later in the session
The 11:15 rule is a pre-committed switch of exit mechanism based on time and decay math — not an emotional close. It is a structured exit.

8. Risk Management

Daily loss rule

One loss = done for the day.
If a trade closes at a loss, no new entries are taken for the remainder of the session. Any open positions are managed to plan only — stops, trails, and TPs remain active.

This rule is completely separate from stop and trail management. A trailing stop triggering on a winner is not a loss. A stop being moved to breakeven is not a loss. The daily loss rule applies only when a position closes negative.

Position sizing

  • Full size: high-conviction setups on tickers whose contract behavior is well understood
  • Monday: 75% of standard — trading on prior week data before fresh price discovery is established. Full size only when a clear new level has printed that morning.
  • 0DTE: 60–75% size, given elevated gamma and decay risk
  • New or unfamiliar ticker: reduced size until spread behavior, fill quality, and delta response are understood
  • Wide spreads: factor spread into effective RR before entry. If spread materially changes the math, size down or skip.

There is no secondary dollar-based daily loss limit. The defined stop on each trade combined with the one-loss rule constitutes the complete daily risk framework.

10. Mental Framework

What this model is

  • Reactive — I respond to what price does at a level, not what I expect it to do
  • Level-based — the trade lives or dies on level quality and entry tightness
  • Rules-based — entry, stop, and TP are fully defined before the trade is on
  • Direction-agnostic — long or short is determined by price structure, not bias

What this model is not

  • Predictive — I am not calling where price goes, I am reacting to what it proves
  • Trend-dependent — counter-trend entries are valid with the right level and tight entry
  • Emotion-driven — all exits are structural: stop hit, TP hit, or trail triggered

Known psychological patterns to guard against

  • Emotional exits on winners — the primary documented P&L leak. No manual close without a structural trigger.
  • Emotional bleed — a loss affecting the next trade. One loss ends the session entirely.
  • FOMO entries — loose or chased entries after a missed setup. A missed trade costs nothing. A bad entry costs capital.
  • Misusing the sub-4min exception — it applies to genuinely strong levels only, not to impatience.
  • Borderline counter-trend entries — if the level needs justification, it is not strong enough.
A missed trade costs nothing. A bad entry costs capital. When the setup is not clearly there, the trade is not there.

11. Trade Review Standard

Every trade receives a written review using this format, posted within 24 hours.

  • IDEA — What was the setup? Name the level, direction, and confluence.
  • ENTRY — Was it tight? How long did the level hold? What confirmed it?
  • EXIT — Was the exit structural (stop, TP, or trail) or emotional? If emotional, what drove it?
  • TAKEAWAY — A few sentences covering what worked, what didn’t, and what to carry forward.
Honest post-trade review is the feedback loop that makes this model improve. Every rule in this document was derived from reviewing trades that broke earlier versions of it.
